Deep Dive: Transaction Importer

Automatically pull payments out of PayPal, Stripe and friends, drop them into Odoo, and match them to the right invoices — with no copy-pasting. A thorough but plain-language guide for a new joiner.

1. What is this?

When a business sells online, money ends up sitting at payment companies — PayPal, Stripe, and so on — each with its own list of payments. The Transaction Importer reaches into those companies, copies each payment into Odoo, and then matches it to the invoice it paid. So your accounting stays up to date on its own.

Two words to get straight right away:

Transaction
Here it means one payment held by a provider — e.g. "customer paid €40 by PayPal on 3 March, PayPal kept €1.20 in fees". (It is not a database thing — it's a real money movement.)
Provider
The outside payment service the money is sitting in. This module knows six of them: PayPal, Stripe, Mollie, Heidelpay, Shopify and SIX (a Swiss card processor).

Why it exists: without it, someone has to download those payment lists and hand-type (or CSV-import) them into Odoo, then manually tick off which payment paid which invoice — slow and easy to get wrong. This module does both halves automatically.

2. The big picture

End to end, a payment travels like this — and every step can run on a timer or be triggered by hand:

Step 1 · Source
Provider
PayPal/Stripe/… holds the payment
→
Step 2 · Fetch
Import
Odoo logs in and downloads the payments for a date range
→
Step 3 · Land
Bank statement line
Each payment becomes a row on a bank statement in Odoo
→
Step 4 · Match
Reconcile
Odoo links each payment to the invoice it settles
Safe to re-run. The importer remembers what it already brought in (it checks each payment's id). If you import an overlapping date range twice, it simply skips the ones it already has — no duplicates.

A few words you'll hear

Integration
One saved connection to one provider account (its login details + schedule). One PayPal account = one integration.
Setting Line
A rule that says "payments matching these conditions go into this accounting book, and match to invoices like this." An integration can have several.
Journal
The accounting book (a specific bank/cash account) where the imported payments land.
Bank statement line
The Odoo record each imported payment becomes — one row of a bank statement (money in or out).
Reconcile
Officially linking a payment to the invoice it pays, so the invoice is marked settled.
Scheduled action (cron)
A background job that runs on a timer. This module makes two per integration — one to import, one to reconcile.
Log
The system's diary of what happened (successes, skips, errors) — your first place to look when something seems off.
Production vs Test
Production = the real, live account. Test (staging) = a safe practice account; the module uses separate "Test Credentials" there automatically.

3. Under the hood (the deep-dive bit)

You don't need this to use it — but it helps you understand what you're clicking:

The Integration record
The heart of it. It holds the provider login and runs the two jobs (import + reconcile). It's built on much.'s shared Integration Hub foundation, so it behaves like our other connectors (same states, same logging).
Setting Lines do the routing
Each line points at a Journal and carries the matching rules. No active line = nothing imports (the log will literally say "No importer setting lines configured").
Matching options
You can require an exact amount match, or allow small over/underpayment tolerances. You also choose to match by Partner (the customer) or by Transaction ID. Matching on neither is risky — it could tie a payment to any invoice with the same amount.
Fees
Optionally, the importer adds one extra statement line that sums the provider's fees, so your books reflect what the provider kept.
Safety nets
Work happens in batches; a bad batch is rolled back without spoiling the others. Long runs pause after ~10 minutes and continue next time. Empty date ranges stop retrying after a few tries so nothing loops forever.
