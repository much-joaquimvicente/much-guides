German Accounting, Applied

The accounting ideas from the Odoo handbook change shape when they hit DATEV. This chapter is the mental model that makes the export files make sense — only the parts that matter for DATEV.

much. GmbH · Odoo 19 · SKR03/04 · slots in after Chapter 3 (Vocabulary)
🧠 The one big idea

Odoo thinks in balanced entries and net lines. DATEV thinks in single gross rows where a small tax key does the VAT maths. The export doesn't translate word-for-word — it collapses each Odoo entry into DATEV's one-row-per-booking, gross-plus-key shape. Learn that one shift and everything downstream clicks.

§ German Accounting, Applied to Odoo

First the German-bookkeeping baseline Odoo already gives you, then the four things that change shape once that data has to go to DATEV.

0 · The German baseline — what Odoo already gives you

Before anything DATEV-specific, these are the German-bookkeeping fundamentals. In Odoo they come from the German localization (l10n_de plus the SKR chart) — you mostly configure them, you don't build them. The job here is knowing where each one lives:

German conceptWhat it isWhere it lives in Odoo
Doppelte Buchführung · Soll / HabenDouble-entry bookkeeping — every posting has equal debits (Soll) and credits (Haben).Every account.move; Odoo enforces the balance. Accounting → Journal Entries.

🧑‍💼

Why this leads into DATEV: under GoBD the official books live with the Steuerberater in DATEV, not in Odoo. Odoo keeps this baseline cleanly — which is exactly what lets it hand the data over. Everything below is where that handover forces Odoo's data into DATEV's shape.

1 · One booking = one row (not a balanced entry)

In Odoo every posting is a balanced journal entry: two or more lines whose debits (Soll) equal credits (Haben). DATEV's Buchungsstapel is different — each business transaction is a single row carrying an amount, a Soll/Haben letter, a Konto and a Gegenkonto. DATEV fills in the other side automatically.

So the export can't copy Odoo's lines one-to-one. For each entry it fixes one account as the Gegenkonto (the move's main account — for a sales invoice, the customer's Debitor), skips that main-account line and the separate tax line, and turns each remaining ordinary line into one gross row booked against that Gegenkonto.

🔎

In the code: the row is assembled in much_account_datev_csv_export/models/core/account_move.py — _get_line_match_values() sets account (Konto), counteraccount (Gegenkonto) and letter = "S" if aml.balance > 0 else "H" (Soll/Haben). _should_ignore_line() drops the main-account line, tax lines, and any line where debit == credit.

2 · DATEV books gross — a tax key does the VAT

In Odoo a taxed invoice has a net revenue line plus a separate tax line. DATEV usually does neither: the row carries the gross amount (net + VAT together, the Umsatz column) and you tag it with a BU-Schlüssel (Buchungsschlüssel / Steuerschlüssel). DATEV reads the key, works out the VAT, and posts it to the correct tax account on its own — no explicit VAT line.

Our export writes the tax-included amount to Umsatz (_get_gross_value() returns abs(amount)) and puts the Odoo tax's DATEV code into the BU column (account.tax.l10n_de_datev_code, via _get_code_correction()). The handful of keys you'll actually see:

BU-SchlüsselMeansAmount in the row
919% domestic VAT (Umsatzsteuer / Vorsteuer)Gross — DATEV splits out the 19%
40The control key — “no automatic tax on this line”Gross, but DATEV adds no VAT
94§13b reverse charge (see below)Net — the exception
💡

Key 40 is the safety default. When a line has no mappable tax, the export falls back to the configured DATEV Key — control_datev_key, default "40" in much_account_datev_base — so DATEV never invents VAT we didn't intend.

3 · The exception: §13b reverse charge is net

Under §13b UStG (reverse charge) — typically an EU or foreign supplier — the German buyer, not the supplier, owes the VAT. The supplier invoices net, so the DATEV row for a reverse-charge booking (BU-Schlüssel 94) carries the net amount, and DATEV books both the input- and output-VAT itself.

This is the one place the “DATEV is gross” rule flips, and getting it wrong is expensive: treat a §13b row as gross and you divide an amount that was never grossed-up — understating the expense and both VAT lines and leaving a suspense line (ticket #55495).

⚠️

How the code knows: a reverse-charge tax is recognised by its invoice tax repartition factors netting to zero — an input-VAT line cancelled by an output-VAT line. _is_reverse_charge_tax() in much_account_datev_csv_import/wizards/base/import_datev_wizard.py checks len(tax_reps) >= 2 and abs(sum(factor_percent)) < 1e-6, and the importer then skips the ÷(1 + rate) it applies to normal gross codes like BU 9.

4 · Account numbers: Sachkonten, Personenkonten & length

DATEV numbers everything, and the digit length is not cosmetic — it decides which number is a G/L account and which is a customer/vendor.

📒 Sachkonto (G/L account)

Fixed length — 4 digits by default (min 4), set once and matched to your SKR chart. Config: length_general_accounts. Example: 8400 revenue.

👤 Personenkonto (Debitor / Kreditor)

Always one digit longer than the G/L length, so it sits “under” the G/L range. Config: length_personal_accounts. Example: 10001 Debitor.

That +1 rule is why a 4-digit chart gives 5-digit customer/vendor numbers. Debitoren (customers) are auto-assigned from 10000, Kreditoren (vendors) from 70000 — the l10n_de_datev_identifier_customer and l10n_de_datev_identifier fields on the partner.

🇩🇪

SKR03 vs SKR04 is just which chart of accounts you picked at company setup — SKR03 is process-/expense-oriented (the older, very common one), SKR04 mirrors the financial statements. Our modules don't choose it; they only need Length General Accounts to match whichever chart the company uses, or the numbers won't line up in DATEV.
