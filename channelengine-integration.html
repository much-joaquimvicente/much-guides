1. First — what is ChannelEngine itself?

ChannelEngine is an outside company and software platform (it is not ours). Its job is to be a single middleman between a business's own systems and the huge online marketplaces where people shop.

A marketplace is a big website where many sellers list their products — think Amazon or eBay. Selling on lots of them is powerful but painful: each one has its own rules, its own screens, and its own way of sending you orders. ChannelEngine hides all that. You connect your systems to ChannelEngine once, and through it you can sell on 1,300+ marketplaces. A few you'll recognise:

AmazoneBaybol.comZalandoKauflandOttoCdiscountGoogle Shopping…and ~1,300 more

ChannelEngine keeps four things in sync across every channel automatically: product info, stock, prices, and orders. It's often called "middleware" — software that sits in the middle and passes messages between two other systems.

2. What the "ChannelEngine Integration" did

Our product was the bridge between Odoo and ChannelEngine. On its own, Odoo (the business's ERP — the system that runs orders, stock, and invoicing) doesn't know anything about ChannelEngine. The integration connected the two so information flowed automatically, in both directions, with no copy-pasting.

3. What flowed where

The easiest way to picture an integration like this is by direction — what Odoo sends out, and what it brings in:

WhatDirectionIn plain words
Products & offersOdoo → outYour product details (name, description, images, barcode) and your "offer" — the price and stock for each channel — are pushed up so the marketplaces can list them.
StockOdoo → outOdoo keeps sending the current "how many are left" so the marketplaces don't sell something you no longer have (that's called overselling).
PricesOdoo → outPrice changes in Odoo are pushed up so every channel shows the right price.
Ordersin → OdooWhen someone buys on a marketplace, that order comes back into Odoo as a normal sales order — ready to pick, pack and invoice.
Shipments & trackingOdoo → outWhen you ship the order in Odoo, the "shipped" status and the tracking number are sent back so the marketplace can tell the customer.
Cancellations & returnsin → OdooIf a marketplace order is cancelled or the customer returns something, that update flows back into Odoo.
Honest note: the exact buttons, menus and settings of our old ChannelEngine module aren't documented here — the code is retired and not in our current codebase. The flows above are how a marketplace integration like this normally works, and match ChannelEngine's own description of what it does.

4. Words you'll hear

Marketplace / Channel
A website where products are sold (Amazon, eBay…). "Channel" is just a more general word for the same thing.
Offer
A product as listed on one channel — i.e. the product plus its price and stock for that specific marketplace.
GTIN / EAN
The product's barcode number. Marketplaces use it to know exactly which product you mean.
Middleware
Software that sits in the middle of two systems and passes messages between them. ChannelEngine is middleware.
Mapping
Matching up two systems' data — e.g. "our category X = the marketplace's category Y" — so information lands in the right place.
Overselling
Selling more than you actually have in stock. Keeping stock in sync is what prevents it.
1P vs 3P
On Amazon: 1P ("first-party") = you sell to Amazon and they resell it; 3P ("third-party") = you sell directly to shoppers on Amazon's marketplace. ChannelEngine can handle both.
Fulfilment
Who actually stores and ships the goods — you (seller-fulfilled) or the marketplace (e.g. Amazon's "FBA", Fulfilled By Amazon).

6. If a customer still uses it

Where it lived in Odoo
Like our other connectors, under the Integration Hub menu — with its own settings, a log (the system's diary of what happened; your first place to look when something seems off), and scheduled jobs that ran on a timer.
The most common tickets
"An order didn't come into Odoo" or "the marketplace shows the wrong stock/price." Both are usually answered by checking the logs and the last scheduled run — the same way you'd troubleshoot the Shopware connector.
When in doubt
Because this product is retired and not in our current codebase, escalate anything tricky to a senior colleague rather than guessing.
