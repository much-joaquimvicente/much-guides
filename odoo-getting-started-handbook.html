★Glossary

👋 Welcome aboard!

Odoo is the software the whole business runs on — sales, projects, support, stock, accounting and more, all in one place. Before you learn any single app, it helps to learn the ground rules: how to log in, move around, create and find records, and use the little panel that follows you everywhere. Learn these once and every Odoo app suddenly feels familiar. Read top to bottom, or jump around with the menu on the left. Nothing to memorise — you'll build the habits by clicking around.

🧠 Read this first — the one big idea

Odoo is modular. It's one database with many apps bolted on — Sales, Inventory, Helpdesk, Accounting… — that all share the same customers, products and users. Because every app is built from the same building blocks, once you learn how one screen works, you know how all of them work. This handbook teaches those shared building blocks.

1 What Odoo Is

A single, connected business system — not a pile of separate programs.

Odoo is an ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning) system: one place where a company manages most of what it does. What makes it special is that all the pieces are connected. Sell a product in Sales and the stock drops in Inventory, an invoice appears in Accounting, and the customer's history updates — automatically. No copy-pasting between tools.

Apps, not programs

Each business area is an app you switch on when you need it. You only see the apps that are installed. A small company might run three; a big one, thirty. They all live in the same database and share the same data.

🧩 Modular

Turn apps on/off as needs change. Start small, grow later.

🔗 Connected

One customer, one product list — shared by every app.

🎯 Consistent

Every app uses the same buttons, views and search. Learn once.

🌐 Web-based

Runs in your browser. Nothing to install on your laptop.

Community vs Enterprise

Odoo comes in two editions. Community is the free, open-source core. Enterprise is a paid subscription that adds more apps and features (accounting automation, studio, more integrations) plus official support. At much. you'll mostly work with Enterprise features.

ℹ️

Where Odoo runs: it can be hosted by Odoo (Odoo Online), on a managed platform (Odoo.sh), or on a company's own servers (on-premise). As a user the screen looks the same everywhere — the difference is who maintains the server.

2 Logging In & the Screen

Your first five minutes: where everything lives on screen.

Getting in

Open the database's web address in your browser, enter your email and password, and you land on your home screen. Bookmark the address. If you forget your password, use Reset Password on the login page (it emails you a link).

The four corners of the screen

Almost every Odoo screen has the same frame. Learn these four spots and you'll never feel lost:

1

▦ Apps menu — top-left

Click the icon (or the current app's name) to open the grid of all your apps. This is how you jump from Sales to Inventory to Helpdesk.

Your main way to switch apps
2

☰ Top menu bar

Inside an app, the bar along the top holds that app's menus (e.g. in Sales: Orders, Products, Reporting, Configuration). It changes with each app.

Navigate within the current app
3

🍞 Breadcrumbs

The trail just under the top bar (e.g. Orders › S00023) shows where you are. Click any earlier step to go back without losing your place.

Always know where you are
4

🔔👤 Right side

Top-right holds the Activities (clock) and Messages (speech bubble) icons, and your user menu (your name/avatar) for preferences and logging out.

Your notifications & profile
💡

Lost? Click the app name (top-left) then your app again — it takes you to that app's home. And breadcrumbs are your friend: click backwards through them instead of the browser's Back button.

3 Apps & Modules

How apps get added, and what "installing" really means.

The Apps app is the store where you browse and install everything Odoo offers. Installing an app switches on its menus, screens and automations across the whole database — for everyone, not just you.

Apps → search a name (e.g. "Project") → Install

App vs module — same thing, different size

📱 App

A big, user-facing area like Sales or CRM. What you see as a tile on your home screen.

🧱 Module

A smaller add-on that extends an app (a specific integration, report or tweak). Same install mechanism.

⚠️

Installing changes the database for everyone. On a real customer system, apps are installed deliberately by the project team — not clicked on to "have a look". Explore freely in a test database; be careful in production.

ℹ️

Some technical modules are hidden until you switch on Developer Mode (Chapter 10) and remove the "Apps" filter. You rarely need those as a new joiner.

4 Views

The same data, shown in different shapes. Switch views with the little icons at the top-right of a list.

A view is just a way of displaying records. The same 200 sales orders can be shown as a spreadsheet-like list, a drag-and-drop board, or a chart — you pick whichever helps right now. Not every screen offers every view; you'll see icons for the ones available.

📄

List

Rows & columns, like a spreadsheet. Great for scanning many records.

🗂️

Kanban

Cards in columns you drag between stages. Great for pipelines & tasks.

📝

Form

One record in full detail. Where you actually read and edit.

📅

Calendar

Records placed on dates. Great for meetings, deadlines, activities.

📊

Pivot

A cross-table to summarise numbers (totals by month, by salesperson…).

📈

Graph

Bar, line or pie chart of the same data.

⏰

Activity

A grid of who owes what follow-up, by type.

🗺️

Map

Records with an address plotted on a map.

💡

Switching view keeps your filters. Filter a list down to "this month's orders", flip to the Graph icon, and you're charting exactly that subset. Views are just different lenses on the same filtered data.

5 Working with Records

Create, edit, save, and the safe ways to get rid of things.

A record is a single item — one customer, one product, one order. You open one in a form view, and everything you do to your data is really "editing a record".

Create & save

1Click New to start a blank record.
2Fill the fields. Bold / highlighted fields are required — Odoo won't save without them.
3Odoo saves as you go: an unsaved record shows a cloud/☁ or the breadcrumb turns into Save (✓) and Discard (✕) buttons. Click ✓ to keep, ✕ to throw away.
⚠️

Don't navigate away with unsaved changes. If you see the Save/Discard buttons, decide first — otherwise Odoo will nag you (or lose the edit). When in doubt, hit Discard to safely undo everything since you opened the record.

The cog / action menu

On most forms a ⚙ Actions menu (or the gear icon) offers extra options for that record:

📑 Duplicate

Copy a record to make a similar one quickly.

🗄️ Archive

Hide a record you no longer use without deleting it. It disappears from normal views but data (and history) stays. The safe choice.

🗑️ Delete

Permanently remove. Often blocked once a record is linked to accounting or confirmed documents.

🔗 Share / Export

Export selected records to a spreadsheet, or generate a shareable link where supported.

ℹ️

Archive, don't delete. If you're unsure, archive. It's reversible (filter for "Archived" and un-archive), and it never breaks links to past documents the way deleting can.

6 Finding Things

The search bar is the most powerful tool on the screen once you know its three jobs.

The bar at the top of any list does far more than text search. Click into it and you'll see three menus drop down:

🔎Search — type a word; Odoo suggests searching by name, customer, reference, etc. Pick which field you meant.
⛃Filters — pre-built conditions like My Orders, This Month, To Invoice. Combine several to narrow down.
▤Group By — stack records into groups (by Salesperson, by Stage, by Month). Turns a flat list into a collapsible summary.

Save a search as a Favorite

Built the perfect filter + grouping? Open Favorites → Save current search, name it, and it's one click next time. You can even set it as your default for that screen, or share it with the team.

💡

A "wrong-looking" list is usually a leftover filter. If records seem missing, check the coloured filter tags in the search bar and click the ✕ on each to clear them. Filters stick as you move around.

7 The Chatter

The panel beside (or below) most forms — the record's built-in history, notes and messaging. You'll use it constantly.

The chatter turns every record into a little conversation and audit trail. Open a customer, an order or a ticket and you'll see it: a message box, a list of followers, and a timeline of everything that ever happened to that record.

Send Message vs Log Note — know the difference

✉️ Send message

Goes out — it emails the followers of the record (often the customer). Use for external communication.

🗒️ Log note

Stays internal — a private note for colleagues. The customer never sees it. Use for "called them, no answer".

⚠️

This is the classic new-joiner trap. Send message can email the customer; Log note does not. If it's an internal comment, always use Log note. Check which tab you're on before you type.

The rest of the chatter

👥 Followers

People who get notified about this record. Add colleagues so they stay in the loop; remove yourself to stop notifications.

⏰ Activities

Schedule a to-do on the record — "Call back Tuesday". More in Chapter 8.

📎 Attachments

Drag files straight onto the chatter to store them with the record.

🕓 Tracked history

Odoo auto-logs important changes ("Stage: New → Won") so you can see who changed what, when.

💡

Use @mentions. Type @ and a colleague's name in a log note to ping them directly — they get a notification without you leaving the record.

8 Activities & Discuss

How Odoo keeps track of what you need to do, and how the team chats.

Activities — your to-do list, attached to real work

An activity is a scheduled to-do linked to a record: a call, an email, a meeting, an upload. Instead of a separate to-do app, the reminder lives on the order/ticket/customer it's about. The clock icon (top-right) shows everything due across all apps — red = overdue, amber = today, grey = later.

1On any record's chatter, click Activities → pick a type, a due date and a note.
2It appears in your clock menu and on the Activity view of that app.
3Done it? Click Mark Done — optionally logging a note and scheduling the next one in one go.

Discuss — the built-in chat

The Discuss app (speech-bubble icon) is Odoo's internal messaging: direct messages, and channels for teams or topics (like Slack/Teams). Messages you're mentioned in, plus follower notifications from records, all gather here so nothing slips through.

💡

Live by the clock icon. Start your day there: clear the red overdue items first. If work isn't scheduled as an activity, it's easy to forget — so schedule the next step before you close a record.

9 Users, Companies & Your Preferences

Who can do what, working across multiple companies, and setting up your own profile.

Users & access rights

Every person is a user. What each user can see and do is controlled by access rights — grouped into roles per app (e.g. in Sales: User vs Administrator). This is why a colleague might see a menu you don't: different rights, same database.

👤 Internal user

A staff member with a proper login and app access. (These are what a subscription counts.)

🌐 Portal user

An external contact (customer/vendor) who only sees their own documents through a limited portal — no back-office access.

Multi-company

One Odoo database can hold several companies. If you have access to more than one, a company switcher appears near your name — tick which companies you're working in. Records belong to a company, so the switcher controls what you see.

Your preferences

Open the user menu (your avatar, top-right) → Preferences / My Profile to set the things that make Odoo yours:

  • Language — the whole interface translates instantly.
  • Timezone — get this right or your times/deadlines will look off.
  • Email signature — added to messages you send from Odoo.
  • Notification preference — handle notifications by email or in-app (Discuss).
ℹ️

Set your timezone first. A wrong timezone is the #1 reason activities and meeting times look one or two hours off for new joiners.

10 Settings & Developer Mode

Where behaviour is configured, and the "show me more" switch consultants use daily.

Settings

The Settings app has a General Settings page plus a section for each installed app. This is where features are switched on/off and defaults are set. Two habits:

1Use the app filter/menu at the left of Settings to jump straight to, say, Sales settings.
2Toggling a setting often reveals more options below it — read before you save.

Developer Mode

Developer (debug) mode unlocks technical information helpful when configuring or troubleshooting: real field names, model names, extra menus, and the ability to inspect automated rules. It changes what you can see, not your data.

Settings → scroll to the bottom of General Settings → Developer Tools → Activate the developer mode

🔍 See field names

Hover a field to get its technical name — essential when writing docs, imports or asking for help.

🐞 The bug menu

A ⚙/bug icon appears in the top bar with tools to view the record's metadata, fields and automations.

⚠️

Developer mode is for looking, not breaking. It exposes powerful technical screens. As a new joiner, use it to read field/model names and understand the setup — don't change technical settings on a customer's live database without guidance.

💡

Turn it off when done (same place → Deactivate) so your day-to-day screens stay clean and simple.
