👋 Welcome aboard!

Odoo Accounting is where the business tracks every euro that comes in and goes out — customer invoices, supplier bills, bank movements, taxes and reports. This handbook walks you through the whole Accounting & Invoicing course, chapter by chapter, written for someone who has never done accounting before. Read top to bottom, or jump around using the menu on the left. Take it slow — you don't need to memorise anything.

🧠 Read this first — the one big idea

When the database was created, someone picked a country. That choice automatically installed a “fiscal localization package” — which loads a ready-made, legally-compliant Chart of Accounts and a set of taxes. So a lot is already done for you. Your real job is to record the daily documents (invoices, bills, payments) correctly — Odoo does the accounting math behind the scenes.

2 Fiscal Localization

A country "starter pack" that makes Odoo comply with your local tax and accounting laws automatically.

A fiscal localization package is a country-specific module Odoo installs based on the company's country. Instead of building accounting rules from scratch, you inherit a package built to match your country's regulations. It includes:

📗 Chart of Accounts

Account structure matching local standards.

🧾 Taxes

Country-specific tax rates and rules.

🔀 Fiscal Positions

Rules that auto-adjust taxes & accounts by situation (see Ch. 9).

📊 Legal Reports

Templates for mandatory tax filings and statements.

How it's installed & changed

When the Accounting app is installed, Odoo detects the company's country and installs the right package automatically — no manual work. To view or change it:

Accounting → Configuration → Settings → Fiscal Localization → Package
⚠️

You can only switch packages before posting any accounting entries. Once real transactions exist, the localization is locked in — this keeps your financial data compliant and consistent.

3 Chart of Accounts

The master list of every account used to record transactions — your company's financial roadmap.

Every account belongs to one of five types. In plain English:

💰
Owns

Assets

What you own — cash, equipment, money customers owe you.

📤
Owes

Liabilities

What you owe — supplier bills, loans, credit cards.

🏛️
Stake

Equity

The owner's share — assets minus liabilities.

📈
Earns

Income

Money the business earns — sales and revenue.

💸
Spends

Expenses

Money spent to operate — rent, salaries, supplies.

Find it & add an account

Accounting → Configuration → Chart of Accounts → Create

Fill three required fields: Code (an identifying number), Account Name, and Type.

Two settings worth knowing

🔗 Reconciliation

Tick this and the account becomes matchable against bank transactions. Only reconcilable accounts appear when matching.

🧾 Default Taxes

Taxes set here apply automatically whenever this account is used in a sale or purchase.

💡

You rarely start from zero. The localization already filled this list with your country's standard accounts. Day-to-day you're reviewing and occasionally adding — not building it all yourself.

4 Journal Entries & Items

How every transaction is actually recorded behind the scenes. This is the engine of accounting — and Odoo runs it for you.

Every financial document — an invoice, a bill, a bank payment, a payslip — becomes a journal entry: the formal bookkeeping record of that transaction. Each entry is made of individual lines called journal items, and every item is either a debit or a credit to a specific account.

The golden rule: debits = credits

Accounting is double-entry: every transaction touches at least two accounts — one goes up, another goes down — and the two sides always balance.

⚖️

For any journal entry, total debits must equal total credits. That's what keeps the books accurate. If they don't match, Odoo won't let you post the entry.

A worked example

A customer pays you €100. Odoo records one entry with two items:

AccountDebitCredit
Bank (money coming in ↑)€100—
Accounts Receivable (customer owes less ↓)—€100

Both sides are €100 — balanced. ✅

What's a "journal"?

A journal is simply a logbook that groups one category of transactions — e.g. the Sales journal (customer invoices), the Purchases journal (vendor bills), the Bank journal, the Cash journal.

💡

You almost never write journal entries by hand. When you post an invoice, bill or payment, Odoo builds the balanced journal entry for you. Understanding this just helps you read reports and know what's happening under the hood.

5 Customer Invoices

A document you send a customer to request payment for what you sold. It creates a receivable — money owed to you.

The invoice lifecycle

Draft→Posted→Paid
  • Draft — a work-in-progress. Edit or delete freely; nothing has hit your accounts yet.
  • Posted (confirmed) — Odoo locks it, assigns an official number (e.g. INV/2025/0001), and creates the journal entry.
  • Paid — the customer's payment has been matched (reconciled) to the invoice.

Create an invoice

Accounting → Customers → Invoices → New
  • Customer — pick who you're billing. Auto-fills their address, payment terms and accounts.
  • Invoice Date — drives the due date and the accounting period.
  • Invoice Lines — one line per item: pick a Product (auto-fills price, description, taxes), set Quantity, Price, Taxes.

Click Confirm to post. This assigns the official number and records the accounting: Accounts Receivable ↑, Income ↑, Tax recorded.

Payment terms

Payment terms define when a customer must pay. Common examples: Immediate, Net 30 (30 days after invoice), installments (e.g. 30% now / 70% end of month), or 2/10 Net 30 (2% off if paid within 10 days). Set a default on the customer so it applies automatically. Configure under Accounting → Configuration → Payment Terms.

Credit notes (refunds & corrections)

A credit note reverses an amount a customer owes — the only legal way to cancel or correct a posted invoice (you can't just delete it). Use it for mistakes, returns, or refunds. On the invoice click Credit Note, then choose Reverse (editable draft, good for partial refunds) or Reverse and Create Invoice (cancel & re-bill).

Send it

Once posted, open the invoice and click Send — by Email (PDF to the customer), Download, or Print.

⚠️

Draft = safe, Posted = official. Do all editing in Draft. To fix a posted invoice, use Reset to Draft (if not yet paid) or issue a credit note — never try to delete it.

6 Vendor Bills

A supplier's invoice to your company — money you owe. The mirror image of a customer invoice.

Vendor BillCustomer Invoice
DirectionMoney you owe (out)Money owed to you (in)
Who issues itYour supplierYou
Accounting effectAccounts Payable ↑Accounts Receivable ↑
Found underAccounting → VendorsAccounting → Customers

Lifecycle

Draft→Posted→Paid

Posting records the debt (Accounts Payable) but moves no money — paying is a separate step (Chapter 7).

Create a bill

Accounting → Vendors → Bills → New
  • Vendor — the supplier.
  • Bill Reference — the supplier's own invoice number. Important: lets Odoo warn you about duplicates.
  • Bill Date (supplier's date) vs Accounting Date (when it hits your books).
  • Lines — products or free-text charges, quantities, prices, taxes.

Four ways bills get into Odoo

1Manual entry — type it in (best for learning).
2Upload/scan a PDF — Odoo's AI reads the document and auto-fills it; you just review.
3Email alias — suppliers email invoices to a dedicated address; Odoo creates drafts automatically.
4From a Purchase Order — a confirmed PO generates a matching draft bill.
⚠️

Always fill the Bill Reference — it stops you paying the same bill twice. And remember: posting ≠ paying.

7 Payments

"Registering a payment" tells Odoo money changed hands and links it to the right invoice or bill.

⬇️ Customer payment

Money you received. Open the invoice → Register Payment.

⬆️ Vendor payment

Money you paid out. Open the bill → Register Payment.

In the pop-up set the Journal (Bank/Cash), Payment Date, Payment Method and Amount (lower it for a partial payment — Odoo tracks the remaining balance), then Create Payment.

"In Payment" vs "Paid" — the two-stage idea

  • Outstanding account — when you register a payment, Odoo parks it in a temporary holding account. The document shows In Payment = "we've noted it."
  • Bank reconciliation — later you match it to the real bank statement line. Only then does it become Paid = "the bank proves it."
ℹ️

This protects accuracy — a check might bounce or a transfer might take days. If everything is stuck at In Payment, you still need to do bank reconciliation (Chapter 8).

Payment methods & batches

Methods: Manual (cash/transfers — the simple default), Checks, SEPA (European bank file), or online providers (Stripe, PayPal). Batch payments bundle many payments into one file/record — handy when the bank shows a single lump sum, so you reconcile the whole batch at once. Enable under Settings → Batch Payments (one payment method per batch).

Follow-ups (chasing overdue invoices)

Automatic reminders to customers with overdue invoices, escalating by levels (gentle → firm → final) based on days overdue. Configure under Accounting → Configuration → Follow-up Levels. Send via email, SMS, WhatsApp, letter or print.

⚠️

Reconcile your bank before sending follow-ups — otherwise you'll chase customers who already paid.

8 Bank & Cash

Getting your real bank activity into Odoo and matching it to your books.

Bank accounts

Each bank account you add creates a journal (its logbook) and a ledger account (its running balance). Add one from the Accounting Dashboard → Search over 26,000 banks, then either Connect (auto-sync) or Record transactions manually.

Bank synchronization

Odoo pulls transactions straight from your bank — no typing. It syncs every 12 hours (click Fetch Transactions to refresh now). Your bank login is never stored on Odoo's servers; secure providers (Plaid, Yodlee, Salt Edge…) handle it. Requires Odoo Enterprise.

No sync? Import a statement file from your bank instead — CSV, OFX or QIF. Odoo flags likely duplicates automatically.

Bank reconciliation

Reconciliation = confirming your bank's records match your Odoo books. Why it matters:

🔍 Catches errorsBefore they pile up
🛡️ Spots fraudUnfamiliar transactions
💵 True cash viewKnow what you have
✅ Stays compliantRequired for most
1Review unmatched lines from the bank.
2Match each to the right invoice, bill or payment.
3Balance anything left over to the correct account.

Reconciliation models (time-savers)

A model is a saved rule for a recurring transaction — build it once, Odoo reuses it. E.g. a Bank Fees model auto-books the bank's charge every time. Models can be manual (a button you click) or automated (fire by themselves on a match). Find them via the bank journal's menu → Reconciliation Models.

Cash

A cash journal tracks physical cash (a till or petty-cash box). Unlike bank journals it must be created manually under Accounting → Configuration → Journals (type: Cash).

9 Taxes & Fiscal Positions

Odoo calculates tax for you, records what's owed, and produces the reports you file with the government.

Add a product to an invoice and Odoo applies the right tax, calculates the amount, creates the accounting record, and keeps a running total for your tax return. Three tax types:

🧾
On sales

Sales

Applied to customer invoices.

🛒
On purchases

Purchase

Applied to vendor bills.

🔧
Internal

None

Used only inside tax groups.

1Turn on any company-wide tax settings your country needs.
2Activate the pre-built taxes from your localization — enable only the ones you use.
3Set default sales & purchase taxes on each product.

Fiscal positions (automatic tax swapping)

A fiscal position is a rule that automatically changes the taxes and accounts on a transaction depending on who the customer is or where they are — domestic vs EU vs export, B2B vs B2C. Without it you'd edit every invoice by hand.

🌍

Example — "EU B2B": enable Detect Automatically → Country Group: Europe, map 21% VAT → 0% VAT and the domestic revenue account → EU revenue account. Now every EU-business invoice is zero-rated automatically. Set it up under Accounting → Configuration → Fiscal Positions. Your localization already ships sensible defaults.

10 Reporting

Automatic summaries of your financial data. You never do the math — Odoo builds these from the invoices, bills and payments you record.

Accounting → Reporting

⚖️ Balance Sheet

A snapshot on one day: what you own vs owe. Always Assets = Liabilities + Equity.

📈 Profit & Loss

A period view: Income − Expenses = Profit or Loss. "Did we make money?"

🧾 Tax Report

Tax collected vs paid — the figures you file with the government.

⏳ Aged Receivable

Money customers owe you, grouped by how overdue.

⏳ Aged Payable

Money you owe suppliers, grouped by how overdue.

📚 General Ledger

Every transaction, by account — the master logbook.

Tools on every report

  • Date filter — choose the period (a "wrong-looking" report is usually just the wrong date range).
  • Comparison — put two periods side by side to see trends.
  • Drill-down — click a line to expand it, all the way down to the original invoice.
  • Export — PDF (print-ready) or XLSX (Excel, for your accountant).

Filing your tax return (the short version)

1Review the tax return and fix any warnings (draft entries, missing bills).
2Lock the period with a Tax Lock Date so figures can't change after reporting.
3Validate — Odoo posts the closing entry and moves the lock date forward.
4Export & file — Odoo prepares it, but you submit to the tax office yourself.
💡

Start with two reports: the Balance Sheet ("what we own vs owe") and the Profit & Loss ("did we make money"). They tell you most of what you need.

11 Multi-Currency

Invoicing, billing and getting paid in currencies other than your company's main currency.

Your main (company) currency is what your books and reports are expressed in. With multi-currency on, you can trade in others (e.g. bill a US client in USD). The key idea: Odoo always stores two values for a foreign transaction — the foreign amount and its company-currency equivalent — so your official books stay in one currency.

Enable it & add currencies

Accounting → Configuration → Settings → Currencies

Turn on multi-currency, then activate the currencies you use under Configuration → Currencies (every currency exists but is off by default).

Exchange rates

An exchange rate is how much one currency is worth in another (1 EUR = 1.08 USD). Enter rates manually, or — recommended — enable Automatic Currency Rates (daily/weekly/monthly) from a source like the European Central Bank.

Exchange gains & losses

Rates move between invoicing and payment. Invoice 1,000 USD worth €920 today; if it's worth €935 when paid, that €15 is an exchange gain (or a loss if less). Odoo posts this automatically to the gain/loss accounts you set once — you never calculate it by hand.

💡

Set up the Exchange Difference journal + gain/loss accounts before invoicing in foreign currencies, and give a foreign bank account its own journal locked to that currency.

12 Analytic Accounting

A parallel way to track where money is earned and spent — by project, department or cost center — without touching your legal books.

📚 Regular accounting

The legal books — required by law. Answers "how much did we earn and spend?"

🏷️ Analytic accounting

Internal labels/tags on transactions. Answers "which project or department did this belong to?"

Adding an analytic tag does not change your legal numbers — it just enriches them. Example: a €1,000 consultant bill still lands in "Consulting Expenses" (legal), but you also tag it Project Alpha to see what that project really cost.

Plans & accounts

  • Analytic Plan = a category, e.g. "Departments" or "Projects".
  • Analytic Account = a value within it (Sales, Marketing, Project Alpha…). Despite the name, it's really a tag, not a legal account.

Tagging (analytic distribution)

On an invoice/bill line, use the Analytic Distribution column — and you can split by percentage (e.g. an electricity bill 60% Warehouse / 40% Head Office). Automate repetitive tagging with Distribution Models. Enable it all under Settings → Analytics → Analytic Accounting.

ℹ️

Start small — one plan (e.g. "Projects") with a few accounts, kept Optional at first. Switch a plan to Mandatory only when the team is ready (it blocks posting until a tag is added).

13 Assets & Deferrals

Three features that spread a lump sum across many months or years, so your books match reality instead of showing one giant spike.

FeatureMoney directionSits on books asExample
Asset / DepreciationYou pay for a long-lived thingAsset you own€27k van over 5 years
Deferred RevenueCustomer pays you up frontLiability (you owe service)€1,200 subscription over 12 months
Deferred ExpenseYou pay a supplier up frontAsset (prepaid benefit)€1,200 insurance over 12 months

Assets & depreciation

A fixed asset is something expensive that lasts years (vehicle, machine). Depreciation spreads its cost across those years instead of expensing it all at once. In Accounting → Assets you create an asset (linkable to its vendor bill), and Odoo builds a depreciation board and posts each slice automatically. Methods: Straight Line (equal amounts — simplest), Declining, or a mix.

Deferred revenues & expenses

Both work the same way: on the invoice/bill line, add a start & end date, and Odoo recognizes a slice each period. Deferred revenue = income received before it's earned (a yearly subscription → €100/month). Deferred expense = a cost paid ahead of the benefit (annual insurance → €100/month). Configure the accounts under Accounting → Configuration → Settings.

💡

One takeaway: enter the document once, tell Odoo the time span, and it quietly posts the monthly slices — keeping your Profit & Loss honest instead of lumpy.

14 Budgets & Year-End Closing

Planning your money ahead of time, then finalizing and locking the year when it's done.

Budgets

A budget is a plan for money: what you expect to earn and spend. Odoo compares plan vs reality so you spot overspending while you can still react. Two kinds: Analytic budgets (per project/department) and Financial budgets (per account, shown on the Profit & Loss). Set up via Accounting → Accounting → Analytic Budgets, then track in the Budget Report.

ColumnPlain meaning
BudgetedWhat you planned.
AchievedWhat actually happened so far.
CommittedAchieved + money already promised (e.g. approved purchase orders).
TheoreticalWhere you should be by today if spending were even — "am I ahead or behind pace?"

Year-end closing

"Closing the year" means finalizing and locking all accounts for the fiscal year, so the figures become official and tamper-proof. The checklist:

1Reconcile every bank account against its statement.
2Finish all invoices, bills and payments for the year.
3Review outstanding receivables & payables (chase the overdue).
4Check taxes in the reports.
5Post adjusting entries (depreciation, deferrals, year-end earnings).
6Lock the period with a lock date.

Lock dates

A lock date is a cutoff: any transaction dated on or before it becomes read-only. Set it under Accounting → Accounting → Lock Dates. It only freezes the past — your work on current dates is unaffected.

⚠️

Reconcile as you go — monthly, not all in December — and set the lock date only once your checklist is complete. Unsure about an adjusting entry like depreciation? Ask your accountant; it affects official figures.

15 E-Invoicing & Online Payments

Sending invoices in a legal machine-readable format, and letting customers pay you online.

Electronic invoicing

An e-invoice is a structured digital file (usually XML) another system can read automatically — not just a PDF a human retypes. Governments increasingly require it (faster tax reporting, less fraud). Odoo supports many regional formats — Factur-X (FR/DE), Peppol/UBL (Europe-wide, free even in Community), XRechnung, NLCIUS. Set the format per customer in their Accounting tab; general settings under Settings → Peppol Electronic Invoicing. Odoo then attaches the right file automatically.

Online payment providers

Services like Stripe and PayPal let customers pay invoices online by card. Odoo marks the invoice paid automatically, and the provider handles the card data — so your business never stores card numbers. Configure under Accounting → Configuration → Payment Providers.

⛔ Disabled

Off — customers can't use it.

🧪 Test mode

Safe sandbox with fake credentials — no real money. Always test here first.

💡

Rule of thumb: online payment providers make money come in faster; e-invoicing keeps you legally compliant in how invoices are sent. Set a provider to Test mode, run a fake payment, then switch to Enabled for real money.
