Procuros Connector — Onboarding Process Map

How Odoo exchanges EDI trade documents with partners over the Procuros network — the business flows and the code behind them.

Why this connector exists

Large retailers and grocery chains do not accept PDF orders or emailed invoices — they trade over EDI (Electronic Data Interchange), where every document is a structured message and every partner, product and pack is a standardised GS1 code (partner = GLN, product = GTIN). Procuros is the network that carries those messages between trading partners. This module is the bridge that lets an Odoo company speak that language: it turns Odoo purchase orders, sales orders, deliveries, invoices, credit notes and payments into Procuros EDI documents and back again — automatically, on a schedule, with full logging.

It is symmetric, so the same module serves two personas depending on which side of a deal you are on:

  • Purchase-to-Pay (P2P) — you are the buyer. You send Orders to a supplier and receive back Shipping Notices, Invoices and Credit Notes; when you pay, a Remittance Advice goes out.
  • Order-to-Cash (O2C) — you are the supplier. You receive Orders from a customer and send Order Responses, Shipping Notices, Invoices and Credit Notes; you receive Remittance Advice when they pay.

Audience for this map: new engineers and support/PM colleagues. Every step below reads at two levels — a plain-English sentence for the business, and the exact Odoo model, method or API path for the engineer. One concrete deal runs through all of it: buyer ACME Co. Ltd. (GLN 1100001016310, Berlin) ordering from supplier Testsupplier (GLN 1100001016312) on order PO9383-R45.

1. The big picture

Odoo sits on one side, the Procuros EDI network (and, behind it, your trading partners) on the other. In between sits this single module, procuros_connector. Everything it does is built from just four kinds of building block, and colour means the block throughout this whole map: the connector & cron that drives it, the GS1 mapper that translates codes to Odoo records, the records & API calls that do the work, and the logs that make every exchange auditable.

2. The architecture

Under the hood the module is two mirror-image lanes driven by scheduled actions. Inbound, the Auto-Process cron calls GET /v2/transactions, then dispatches each document to a handler by its type string (process_incoming_order, process_incoming_invoice, …), resolving GS1 codes to Odoo records as it goes. Outbound, each Odoo document serialises itself with a generate_*_payload() method and is POSTed back. A single connector.configuration record plus three ir.cron jobs are the engine; connector.log records every step and the Auto-Mark cron acknowledges handled transactions back to Procuros so they are never re-sent.
