👋 Welcome aboard!

Odoo Subscriptions handles anything a customer pays for repeatedly — monthly or yearly. It is a sales order that keeps going: it renews on schedule, invoices automatically, and can even charge a saved card. It also gives you the recurring-revenue metrics (MRR, churn) that subscription businesses live by.

🧠 Read this first — the one big idea

A subscription is a sales order that repeats. Put a recurring product on an order with a recurrence (e.g. monthly), confirm it, and Odoo generates invoices every period and renews it until someone stops it. Everything else is managing that cycle.

1 What Subscriptions Are

Sell once, get paid on a repeating schedule.

A subscription bills a customer again and again on a set rhythm — perfect for SaaS, memberships, maintenance plans or retainers. Odoo automates the boring part: creating each period’s invoice, collecting payment, and renewing the contract, while tracking the health of your recurring revenue.

🔁 Subscription

A recurring sales order.

⏱️ Recurrence

How often it renews/bills (monthly, yearly…).

📈 MRR

Monthly Recurring Revenue — the headline metric.

📉 Churn

Customers leaving — lost recurring revenue.

2 Recurring Products & Plans

The building blocks: a price that repeats, and how often.

A recurring product carries a price meant to be charged each period. A recurrence / subscription plan defines the rhythm — Monthly, Quarterly, Yearly — and rules like duration and how it self-renews or self-closes.

ℹ️

The same product can be offered on several recurrences (e.g. cheaper per month if billed yearly) via pricing.

4 The Subscription Lifecycle

A subscription has a life of its own after confirmation.

1In Progress — active and invoicing each period.
2Renewed — extended for another term (kept the customer).
3Churned — the customer left; recurring revenue is lost.
4Closed — ended and no longer billing.
ℹ️

Renewal and churn are the two events that move MRR up or down — the whole business is about maximising the first and minimising the second.

5 Recurring Invoicing & Payment

The part that runs without you.

On each period, Odoo generates the invoice automatically. If the customer saved a payment token (a stored card via a payment provider), Odoo can charge it automatically and mark the invoice paid — true hands-off recurring billing. Failed payments can trigger reminders.

💡

Automatic payment tokens are what turn subscriptions from “remember to invoice” into a self-running revenue stream.

6 Growing & Keeping Subscriptions

The levers that move recurring revenue.

⬆️ Upsell

Add products or upgrade the plan mid-term — MRR goes up.

⬇️ Downgrade

Reduce the plan — MRR goes down but you keep the customer.

🔁 Renewal

Extend the term at period end.

📉 Churn

A cancellation — the number to keep low.

Odoo tracks these events so you can see why MRR changed, not just that it did.

7 Automation & Reporting

Set rules once; read the health of the book of business.

Subscription plans can auto-renew, auto-close after a set time, or send alerts (e.g. “expiring soon”). Reporting centres on recurring-revenue metrics:

📈 MRR / ARR

Monthly / annual recurring revenue.

📊 Retention

Share of customers/revenue kept over time.

📉 Churn analysis

Where and why revenue is lost.

🔮 Forecast

Expected recurring revenue ahead.
