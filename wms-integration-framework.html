1. First — what is a WMS?

Let's unpack the jargon before anything else.

WMS stands for Warehouse Management System — the software that runs a physical warehouse. It decides where each item is stored, and it guides the workers through picking (grabbing the items for an order off the shelves), packing (putting them in a box), and shipping (handing the box to a courier).

Often the warehouse isn't even the customer's own — it's a 3PL (Third-Party Logistics provider): an outside company you pay to store your goods and ship your orders for you. Either way, that warehouse runs its own WMS software, separate from Odoo.

The core problem: Odoo knows about orders and stock on paper (the accounting and admin side). The warehouse/WMS knows about the physical goods (what's really on the shelf, what actually shipped). If they don't talk to each other, the two drift apart — Odoo thinks something shipped that didn't, or shows stock that's really gone. An integration keeps them in step.

2. What the "WMS Integration Framework" was

It was much.'s older base layer — a shared foundation — for connecting Odoo to outside warehouse/3PL systems. A "framework" here means the common plumbing: the boring-but-essential machinery (running tasks on a timer, keeping logs, matching data between two systems, handling errors) that every individual warehouse connector needs. Instead of rebuilding that plumbing for each new warehouse, connectors were built on top of this framework.

3. What flowed where

These integrations are easiest to understand by direction. There are two main journeys — goods going out to customers, and goods coming in from suppliers — plus keeping stock honest.

WhatDirectionIn plain words
Delivery ordersOdoo → warehouseWhen a customer order needs shipping, Odoo tells the warehouse "please pick, pack and send this."
Shipment confirmation + trackingwarehouse → OdooOnce the warehouse ships it, it reports back "done, here's the tracking number" — and Odoo marks the delivery complete.
Expected receipts (ASN)Odoo → warehouseWhen new stock is on its way from a supplier, Odoo warns the warehouse in advance so they're ready to receive it. This heads-up is called an ASN.
Received quantitieswarehouse → OdooWhen the goods physically arrive and are counted, the warehouse reports the real quantities back, and Odoo's stock goes up.
Stock levelswarehouse → OdooThe warehouse is the source of truth for what's physically there, so it periodically syncs the real stock back to Odoo to prevent overselling.
Returnsboth waysA customer return is registered and the stock is put back — kept in step between the two systems.
Honest note: the exact screens, fields and settings of our old WMS framework aren't documented here — the code is retired and not in our current codebase. The flows above are how warehouse/3PL integrations generally work; the specifics varied per connector built on the framework.

4. Words you'll hear

WMS (Warehouse Management System)
Software that runs a warehouse — storage, picking, packing, shipping.
3PL (Third-Party Logistics)
An outside company you pay to store and ship your goods for you.
ASN (Advance Shipping Notice)
A "heads-up" message that stock is on its way, so the receiving side can prepare.
Pick / Pack / Ship
The three warehouse steps: grab the items, box them, send them.
SSCC
A standard barcode number that identifies one specific pallet or carton, so it can be tracked as a unit.
Master data
The shared "facts" both systems must agree on — mainly the product list — so a message about "product X" means the same thing on both sides.
Framework / base layer
Shared plumbing that other, more specific connectors are built on top of, so common work isn't rebuilt each time.

5. Why it's retired — and what to use instead

It has been replaced by a newer, cleaner foundation called Order Base, which itself sits on the Integration Hub (our current shared base for all integrations). Because the newer framework does the same job better, the old WMS framework was retired: no new features, no new sales.

For any new project, start here instead: Order Base — our current integration framework. There's a proper guide for it: Integration Hub & Order Base. Never begin new work on the retired framework.

6. If a customer still uses it

What it does day to day
It quietly moves information between Odoo and an outside warehouse — telling the warehouse to ship orders, and hearing back when they're shipped or received.
Where to look when something breaks
The logs (the system's diary of what happened) are your first stop, just like every other connector. Check the last scheduled run too — a message may simply not have been sent or received yet.
Typical tickets
"An order wasn't sent to the warehouse", "a shipment didn't come back into Odoo", or "stock doesn't match" — all usually traced through the logs.
When in doubt
Because this framework is retired and not in our current codebase, escalate anything tricky to a senior colleague — and steer any new requirements toward Order Base.
