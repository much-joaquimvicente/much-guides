★Glossary

👋 Welcome aboard!

Odoo Inventory tracks physical goods: what you have, where it is, and every move it makes. It underpins Sales, Purchase and our warehouse integrations (Shopware, Order Base). The core idea is beautifully simple once it clicks — stock is only ever moved between locations.

🧠 Read this first — the one big idea

Stock is never created or destroyed — only moved. Every receipt, delivery or adjustment is a move of product from one location to another (even “Vendors” and “Customers” are locations). Get this and all of Inventory makes sense.

1 What Inventory Is

Always know how much of each product you have, and where.

Inventory keeps a live count of stock across warehouses and the locations inside them (shelves, zones). Every physical event — a supplier delivery, a customer shipment, moving goods between shelves — is recorded so the numbers stay accurate without manual tallies.

🏭 Warehouse

A physical site that holds stock.

📍 Location

A precise spot inside (or outside) a warehouse.

🔄 Move

Stock going from one location to another.

📦 Operation

A grouped set of moves: receipt, delivery, transfer.

2 Products & Stock Types

Only some products are actually counted.

📦

Storable

Stock is tracked — counts go up and down. The real inventory items.

🧰

Consumable

Handled but not counted (e.g. packing tape).

🛎️

Service

No physical stock at all (e.g. a support hour).

Each product also has a unit of measure (pieces, kg, litres). Odoo can even convert between buying and selling units.

ℹ️

If a product’s stock “isn’t moving”, check its type — only storable products are tracked.

3 Operations — Receipts, Deliveries, Transfers

The three everyday jobs, all visible on the Inventory dashboard.

📥

Receipts

Goods coming IN from a supplier.

📤

Deliveries

Goods going OUT to a customer.

🔀

Internal Transfers

Goods moving between your own locations.

The Inventory Overview dashboard shows a card per operation type with a “to process” count. Click in, open a transfer, and Validate it to confirm the goods actually moved.

4 The Double-Entry Stock Model

The one concept that explains everything else.

Just like accounting balances debits and credits, Inventory balances every move between two locations. Stock is never invented — it comes from somewhere and goes to somewhere. Odoo models even the outside world as locations:

EventFrom locationTo location
ReceiptVendorsStock
DeliveryStockCustomers
Internal transferShelf AShelf B
ScrapStockScrap
💡

Because “Vendors” and “Customers” are locations too, every unit is always accounted for — this is what makes Odoo’s stock traceable end-to-end.

5 Replenishment

Never run out — let Odoo tell you when to buy or make more.

A reordering rule sets a minimum and maximum for a product at a location. When stock drops below the minimum, Odoo proposes replenishing back up to the maximum (a purchase order or manufacturing order). The Replenishment screen lists everything that needs ordering.

ℹ️

Set realistic lead times (how long a supplier takes) so reordering triggers early enough to avoid stockouts.

6 Traceability — Lots & Serials

Track exactly which unit went where.

🔢 Lot numbers

A batch shares one number — good for food, pharma, batches.

#️⃣ Serial numbers

Every single unit gets its own unique number.

🧭 Traceability report

Follow a lot/serial’s whole journey in and out.

⏳ Expiry

Optionally track best-before dates on lots.

Turn tracking on per product. It’s essential where you must prove where a specific unit came from or went.

7 Counts, Adjustments & Reporting

Keep the numbers honest and read the stock story.

Real shelves drift from the system. A physical inventory / count lets you enter the counted quantity and Odoo posts an adjustment move to reconcile. Reporting then answers the big questions:

📊 On-hand

What you physically have now, by location.

🔮 Forecasted

Future stock, factoring incoming and outgoing orders.

💰 Valuation

What your stock is worth — feeds accounting.

📚 Moves History

Every move ever, fully searchable.
